# claude_3ch.rmt, claude_3ch_fast.rmt – how the 3 channel songs were made

`music/claude_3ch.rmt` and `music/claude_3ch_fast.rmt` were composed and
written by Claude (Claude Code, model Opus 5.5) from requests in a chat
session. No MOD, MIDI or audio
file was converted: the notes, the instruments and the RMT module layout are
generated by `tools/song3ch.py`, which Claude wrote in the same session.

## The prompts

The session started by checking how the C64 player (`../rmt_cbm64`) sounds
with `gemx.rmt`. The C64 audio recorded in VICE matched the Atari rendering
of `tools/rmtplay` with a pitch correlation of 0.67, because the SID has 3
voices and drops the quieter of POKEY channels 3 and 4. Then the user asked
(verbatim, in Italian):

> sarebbe carino utilizzare lo stesso metro di paragone. 3 canali su tutti i
> player per non avere differenze. Sei in grado di creare una melodia RMT
> accattivante con solo 3 canali (1+2 e 3)? Cioe' dove il canale 4 e' sempre
> muto?

("it would be nice to use the same yardstick: 3 channels on every player, so
there are no differences. Can you create a catchy RMT melody with only 3
channels (1+2 and 3)? That is, where channel 4 is always silent?")

and, after listening to it in VICE:

> aggiorna il README e fai il commit anche del brano rmt che hai creato e
> chiamalo claude_3ch.rmt

The second song came from (after the first one had been played on atari800
with PokeyATest):

> crea un altro brano a 3 canali più veloce

("create another, faster 3 channel song")

Later, after the C64 player with visualizer (`../rmt_cbm64/rmtplay.sh`)
was written:

> vorrei che la metadati dei file rmt generati da te siano lunghi abbastanza
> da essere scrollati

("I would like the metadata of the rmt files you generated to be long enough
to scroll")

There was no other prompt or hidden specification: the choices below were
made by Claude.

## Choices made from the request

| Requirement | Choice |
|---|---|
| channel 4 always silent | every song line has track `$FF` on channel 4 |
| same result on every player | only features that `../rmt_cbm64/src/sidrmt.s` maps to the SID: pure tone, distortion C, poly17 noise, no 16 bit bass, no filter, no AUDCTL, no volume only |
| "1+2 and 3" | the main voices (lead, bass) on channels 1+2, which keep playing on the Atari during disk loading; drums and chords on channel 3 |
| catchy | a short hook in A minor over the vi-IV-I-V progression (Am F C G), a B part (Dm Em F G, then Dm Em F E back to A minor), octave bass, kick/snare backbeat, chord stabs between the drums |
| RMT4 for this tree | instrument speed 1, track length 64, module layout of `tools/mod2rmt4.py` (song last, as `tools/rmt2ca65.py` expects) |

## The song

- A minor, speed 6 (125 BPM at 50 Hz), 16 rows per bar, 4 bars per track
- song lines: intro (bass + drums), intro with stabs, A, A', B, B', A, A';
  after the last line it goes back to line 2 (about 61 s per pass)
- channel 1 lead: pure tone (AUDC `$A0`), quick attack, decay to a sustain,
  vibrato after 10 frames; rests are a volume 0 event
- channel 2 bass: distortion C (`$C0`), eighth notes alternating root and
  octave; each note uses the bass table (1 or 2) closest in pitch
- channel 3: kick (distortion C, AUDF sweep 120 → 45 Hz) on beats 1 and 3,
  snare (poly17 noise, AUDF 6 → 20) on 2 and 4, and in between a 13 frame
  chord stab: pure tone with the instrument table playing 0-3-7 (minor) or
  0-4-7 (major) one note per frame
- pitch error of the frequency tables: at most 23 cents (bass), 26 cents
  (lead and stabs)

## The fast song (claude_3ch_fast)

Same generator, instruments and channel layout; what changes is in its
`SONGS` entry:

- E minor, speed 4 (188 BPM at 50 Hz), about 41 s per pass, loops to line 2
- A part: Em C G D (i-VI-III-VII); B part: Am Em C D, then Am Em C B, the
  B major chord (with D#) leading back to E minor
- new melody, with 16th note pickups at the start of the B bars
- channel 3 pattern `drive`: an extra kick on the "and" of beat 2
  (rows 0, 6, 8 kick, 4, 12 snare, 2, 10, 14 chord stabs)
- pitch error: at most 18 cents (bass), 26 cents (lead and stabs)

## Song text (metadata)

Like the files saved by the RMT editor, both modules have a second block
after the module: the song text, then the name of every instrument, each
ending with a 0 byte. The music block is unchanged by it. Players that show
the song info split the text at the first ", " (`tools/rmtinfo.py` in
`../rmt_cbm64` and in VERA_ATARI_PBI):

| Song | NAME | AUTHOR |
|---|---|---|
| `claude_3ch` | Claude 3ch - an original song in A minor for POKEY channels 1-3 and the C64 SID (79 characters) | composed by Claude (Opus 5.5) with RmtSkeleton/tools/song3ch.py in 2026 (72) |
| `claude_3ch_fast` | Claude 3ch fast - an original song in E minor at 188 BPM for POKEY channels 1-3 and the C64 SID (97) | the same (72) |

Both lines are longer than the 40 columns, so they scroll. DATE stays empty:
the year is already on the AUTHOR line. Instrument names: lead, bass
(table 1), bass (table 2), kick, snare, stab minor 0-3-7, stab major 0-4-7.
The text is the `text` field of each `SONGS` entry.

## How it was checked

- `python3 tools/rmt2ca65.py music/claude_3ch.rmt /dev/null`: valid, instr. speed 1
- `tools/rmtplay/rmtplay -t 70 -w out.wav`: the strongest pitch classes of
  every bar are the notes of the planned chord (36 bars)
- C64: `../rmt_cbm64` built with `SONG=music/claude_3ch.rmt`, audio recorded
  from VICE 3.9 (`x64sc`, PAL, reSID 6581): chroma correlation with the
  rmtplay rendering 0.85 (0.83–0.94 on 10 s windows, -0.07 or less when
  transposed), against 0.67 for `gemx.rmt`
- builds with `SONG=music/claude_3ch.rmt` in RmtSkeleton, PokeyATest and
  rmt_cbm64
- `claude_3ch_fast`: the same checks; chords right in all 36 bars,
  C64/rmtplay correlation 0.80 (0.80–0.92 on 10 s windows, -0.08 or less
  when transposed), builds in PokeyATest and rmt_cbm64
- with the song text added, the first block (the module) is byte for byte
  the one before; `rmt2ca65.py` and `rmtplay` read it as before; in
  `../rmt_cbm64/rmtplay.sh` both info lines scroll (checked in VICE)

Whether the melodies are catchy was left to the user's ears: the checks above
cover pitch, harmony and timing only.

## Changing it

Each song is an entry of `SONGS` in `tools/song3ch.py`: speed, chord
progressions (`prog`), melodies (`melody`, one string per bar,
`row:note:length`), song lines (`lines`: melody, progression, bass style,
channel 3 pattern) and loop line. Chords are in `CHORDS`, channel 3 patterns
in `CH3`; instruments and volumes are shared by all songs. A new entry is a
new song. Then:

```bash
python3 tools/song3ch.py music/claude_3ch.rmt
python3 tools/song3ch.py --song claude_3ch_fast music/claude_3ch_fast.rmt
cp music/claude_3ch*.rmt ../PokeyATest/music/
cp music/claude_3ch*.rmt ../rmt_cbm64/music/
```
